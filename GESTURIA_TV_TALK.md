# GESTURIA — The 5-Minute Talk (TV / interview / presentation)

*Speak it, don't read it. Short sentences. Let the interpreter do half the talking.
Every claim in here is real and running — nothing is a promise.*

---

## BEFORE YOU GO ON (2 minutes, backstage)

1. Run **START_GESTURIA.ps1** → wait for **"GESTURIA IS LIVE"**.
2. Open **/studio**, pick your mic language (EN or FR), say one test sentence.
3. Say *"Welcome to Gesturia"* once — confirm he signs and the golden caption follows.
4. Set signing speed to **1×** (it auto-accelerates when you talk fast).
5. Speak in **short sentences with small pauses** — that's how real interpreters are spoken to,
   and it keeps him right behind you.

---

## THE TALK (~5 minutes)

### 1 · Open with the machine, not with yourself (0:00–0:40)

*(Face the screen. Speak to it, not about it.)*

> "Good morning, Cameroon."

*(He signs it. Point at the caption.)*

> "Watch the golden word — that is the sign his hands are making, at this exact moment.
> He is not a recording. He is translating **me** — live — as I speak.
> And he does it in our two languages."

*(Switch to French, one sentence:)*

> "Bonjour — trois cent mille Camerounais parlent cette langue."

*(Let him finish. Then turn to the presenter/audience.)*

### 2 · The name is the story (0:40–1:10)

> "His home is called **Gesturia**. From the word *gesture* — a country of gestures.
> Listen — when I say the name… *'Gesturia'* … he makes the sign for *gesture*. The name,
> spoken, becomes the language itself.
>
> We built Gesturia here in Cameroon. It runs on an ordinary computer. It does not
> even need the internet."

### 3 · Why it exists — the human cost (1:10–2:00)

> "Here is the reality. More than **three hundred thousand Cameroonians are deaf**.
> They have a full, beautiful language — but almost nobody around them speaks it.
>
> So a deaf child sits in class, and the lesson passes over her head.
> A deaf man stands before a doctor and cannot say where it hurts.
> A deaf woman comes to an office and is handed a paper instead of a conversation.
> And when the nation speaks — on a broadcast like this one — they watch lips move.
>
> That is not a disability problem. That is a **translation** problem.
> And translation problems can be solved."

### 4 · What Gesturia actually does — today (2:00–3:30)

> "So what does Gesturia do? Five things — and all five are working today, not in a plan.
>
> **It interprets.** What you see now: speech or writing, English or French, becomes
> sign language in real time. Over **three thousand six hundred signs**, and growing.
>
> **It teaches.** Anyone — a teacher, a nurse, a parent of a deaf child — can learn to
> sign, step by step, like a language app.
>
> **It corrects you.** Turn on your camera, perform a sign, and Gesturia watches your
> hands and tells you what to fix — like a patient teacher who never gets tired.
> And when you've earned it, it gives you a **certificate**.
>
> **It runs a classroom.** A teacher speaks; every deaf student sees the lesson signed,
> live, with notes captured for study. One teacher — every child included.
>
> **And it learns from the deaf themselves.** This is my favourite part. A deaf
> Cameroonian performs a sign to the camera — and Gesturia learns it. Sign by sign,
> the community is building the first living dictionary of Cameroonian sign language.
> It is not our gift to them. It is **their language — becoming national infrastructure**."

### 5 · What it means for Cameroon (3:30–4:30)

> "Now picture where this goes.
>
> In **schools** — deaf children following the same lesson as everyone else, and passing
> the same exams.
> In **hospitals** — a patient describing pain in their own language, and being treated,
> not guessed at.
> In **public offices** — a citizen served with dignity.
> On **television** — this interpreter, in the corner of the screen, on the news, on
> national addresses, so that when Cameroon speaks, **every** Cameroonian hears.
> And for deaf adults — a new profession: teaching their language, certifying it,
> growing it.
>
> Every country that takes its languages seriously builds dictionaries, schools and
> broadcasting for them. Gesturia is Cameroon doing exactly that — for the language
> we have ignored the longest."

### 6 · Close (4:30–5:00)

> "I did not build Gesturia because it was easy. I built it because I believe being
> deaf in Cameroon should never again mean being outside the conversation.
>
> The technology exists. The dictionary is alive. The interpreter is standing right
> there — and he is ready to work.
>
> **Gesturia. A country of gestures. Every sign belongs somewhere — and every
> Cameroonian deserves to be understood.**"

*(Optional mic-drop: turn back to the screen —)*

> "Merci, le Cameroun."

*(He signs THANK YOU + fingerspells C-A-M-E-R-O-O-N. Hold the silence. Done.)*

## LIKELY QUESTIONS — grounded answers

**"Is this real, or a demonstration?"**
> "You just watched it translate me live. Ask it something yourself." *(Hand over the
> mic — the strongest moment available on television.)*

**"How many signs does it know?"**
> "Over three thousand six hundred — and when a word has no sign yet, it spells it
> letter by letter, exactly as human interpreters do. You saw it spell *Cameroon*."

**"Who checks that the signs are correct?"**
> "The deaf community. They add and correct signs themselves, through the camera.
> This dictionary belongs to them."

**"Does it replace human interpreters?"**
> "No — it reaches where there are none: the classroom at 8am, the clinic at midnight,
> the broadcast no one budgeted an interpreter for. And it *trains* new interpreters.
> It multiplies access; it doesn't replace people."

**"What do you need now?"**
> "Three things: schools to pilot it, one broadcaster to put it on air — and deaf
> Cameroonians to keep teaching it. Every sign they add serves the whole country."

**"Why did you build this?"**
> *(One personal sentence — yours. That's the answer people will remember.)*

## STAGE NOTES

- **Let him lag.** He finishes a few seconds after you — like every human interpreter
  on Earth. Don't wait for him; the karaoke caption shows he's faithful.
- If recognition mishears a word, **keep talking** — the intelligence corrects typos in
  context and spells what it cannot match. Never apologise for the machine.
- Names are safe: *Cameroon, CRTV, Yaoundé, Douala* are always spelled letter-by-letter,
  never guessed.
- If the screen ever freezes: **START_GESTURIA.ps1**, 90 seconds, back on air.
